# Images, icons, and logos

Choose the deck's visual family before gathering individual assets. A coherent set matters more than finding a different attractive style for each slide.

## One family, authentic marks

- Use one icon library and one stroke, fill, and weight treatment. Avoid mixing a line icon, a shaded 3D object, and a sketch as equivalent elements.
- Use one illustration collection when illustrations are needed. Do not improvise a drawing as filler.
- Use official brand kits or repositories for third-party logos. A unified logo collection is acceptable when its marks are authentic, current, and licensed for the intended use. Verify disputed or malformed marks against the official source.
- Do not invent initials, redraw a company logo, or recolor it to match the deck without an approved brand variant. A consistent layout does not require every brand to have identical colors.

## Optical sizing

Prefer transparent PNGs or a correctly rendered transparent SVG. Keep proportions and brand clear space. Avoid a default white square around every logo unless the series or contrast requirement calls for one.

Match the apparent size of the occupied mark, not the dimensions of its file or an equal square box. Wide wordmarks and compact symbols need different width and height settings. Remove excess transparent file padding only within the brand's allowed clear space. Review all marks side by side on their final backgrounds.

If an SVG looks wrong after nesting or import, inspect its `viewBox`, clipping, and sizing. Use an official high-resolution transparent PNG when it renders more reliably. Do not patch a malformed logo by approximating its shapes.

## Photography

Use Unsplash or another suitable licensed provider, or approved official media. Verify the asset's license; search visibility is not permission. Record the source page and creator. Keep paid or restricted assets out of a free-resource brief.

Choose relevant subjects. An Uber card should show rideshare context; a Stripe card should show payments or a suitable official company scene. A generic laptop is not automatically a meaningful image for every software service.

Keep treatment consistent across the deck: crop logic, tonal range, photo prominence, and overlays. Avoid reusing the same hero photo across recent posts. A deliberately repeated brand element is different from an accidentally reused stock image.

Default to real assets for this workflow. Do not introduce generated artwork when the user asked for real, free-resource, or non-AI images. If the current brief requests generated art, follow it and identify its origin.

## Provenance

Keep a source manifest with the slide, local filename, original URL, creator or owner, usage terms, any crop or variant, and date checked. Record research claims separately from image credits. Never ship private asset locations or credentials in a public handoff.

Prefer a source manifest or unobtrusive credit over stuffing every card with URLs. Put clickable destinations in the caption, prepared message, or link pack when they are the useful next step. Do not imply that a raster slide contains a clickable link.
