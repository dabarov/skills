# Example: make a carousel worth saving

This is an original layout example for the repository, not a published post or a completed Figma file. It uses editorial guidance rather than a ranked dataset. No third-party photos, logos, or illustrations are bundled.

## Brief

- Audience: a creator making a first educational carousel.
- Problem: too many ideas compete on one small screen.
- Payoff: turn one useful point into a readable sequence.
- Angle: show how to make a carousel worth saving in five slides.
- Format: five 1080 × 1350 slides, including the cover and close.
- Visual system: navy, white, and blue; Space Grotesk; text and precise geometry.
- CTA: save the five checks for the next draft. No DM offer is promised.

## Storyboard

| Slide | Job | Headline | Supporting copy | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Make a carousel worth saving. | One useful idea. Enough space to read it. | A clear display headline and a small five-page sequence. |
| 2 | Focus | Start with one problem. | Who is reading? What are they trying to do? What should they leave with? | Three separated questions with one continuous left alignment. |
| 3 | Edit | Keep the strongest parts. | Cut repeats. Group related ideas. Leave room for the close. | A concise list and a visibly separated ten-slide budget. |
| 4 | Explain | Show what the reader needs. | Code for an example. A table for a comparison. A relevant photo for context. | Three typographic rows, without improvised illustrations. |
| 5 | Close | Save these checks for your next draft. | One idea. Clear evidence. One visual family. Readable spacing. A useful next step. | A five-item checklist and one primary save prompt. |

The opening identifies the outcome. The content slides each make a different decision easier. The close repeats the checks without inventing a giveaway or asking for five engagement actions.

## Inspect and reuse

The [README preview](assets/carousel-example.png) shows slides 1, 2, and 5. The source artwork and full-size PNGs are in [example/](example/). SVG text remains editable; PNGs are flattened. These sources can be adapted or placed in Figma. Native editable Figma text would need a separate authoring step.

At the original size, the slides use a 64 px inset. A phone-size view should preserve the title and main copy. Review each real export before publishing, especially after changing a font or adding a longer headline.

## Captions to adapt

**Instagram**

**Threads**

The hardest part of a carousel is choosing what to leave out. Start with one problem, cut repeated advice, and make the last slide useful. What do you usually cut from a first draft?

These are writing examples, not claims about ranking algorithms. Recheck current publishing rules and the final media variant before using them.

## Alt text

1. Make a carousel worth saving: one useful idea with enough space to read it.
2. Three planning questions: who is reading, what are they trying to do, and what should they leave with?
3. Editing guidance: cut repeats, group related ideas, and count the cover and close within a ten-slide limit.
4. Match the visual to the content: code for examples, a table for comparisons, and a relevant photo for context.
5. Save five checks: one idea, clear evidence, one visual family, readable spacing, and a useful next step.
